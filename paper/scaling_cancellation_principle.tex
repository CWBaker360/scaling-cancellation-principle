\title{A Scaling--Cancellation Principle for Chord-Based Geometric Approximations}
\author{C. Wayne Baker}
\date{June 28, 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note separates two geometric error-suppression mechanisms that arise in
chord-based approximation and angular refinement. The first is
scaling--cancellation: when fixed-resolution approximants satisfy an
even-power expansion
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
C_6N^{-6}
+
\cdots,
\]
Richardson-type linear combinations of \(Q_N,Q_{kN},\ldots\) cancel leading
terms and increase the order of accuracy. This mechanism applies to
polygonal approximations of \(\pi\), chordal perimeter approximations, and
geometric N-series acceleration.

The second mechanism is nonlinear residual-map iteration. In the Baker
\(4{:}3\) trisection cascade, the active variable is not a resolution scale
but the signed angular error, and the local map satisfies
\[
e_{j+1}
=
-\frac{7}{384}e_j^3
+
O(e_j^5).
\]
Thus the cascade multiplies error order under iteration, producing
\[
3
\longrightarrow
9
\longrightarrow
27
\longrightarrow
81
\longrightarrow
\cdots.
\]

The contribution of the note is therefore taxonomic rather than a claim of
new Richardson theory. Scaling--cancellation is an order-increasing
fixed-resolution mechanism; the Baker \(4{:}3\) cascade is an
order-multiplying nonlinear refinement. Both suppress leading error terms by
geometrically selected coefficients or scalings, but they act in different
variables and belong to different convergence classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Chord-based geometric approximation appears throughout classical and modern
geometry. Archimedes' polygonal approximation to \(\pi\), chordal perimeter
approximations for ellipses, and perimeter approximations for smooth convex
curves all involve a discretization parameter \(N\). As \(N\) grows, the
approximation improves, and the leading error often has a predictable
asymptotic structure.

A recurring form is
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
C_6N^{-6}
+
\cdots ,
\]
where \(Q\) is the target geometric quantity and \(Q_N\) is its
\(N\)-resolution approximation. The even powers arise from symmetry: local
odd-order errors cancel, leaving a hierarchy in powers of \(N^{-2}\).

The purpose of this paper is to isolate the elementary but useful
consequence of this structure. If \(Q_N\) and \(Q_{kN}\) are available, then
their leading errors differ by the factor \(k^{-2}\). Therefore a specific
linear combination cancels the leading term. This is the geometric form of a
Richardson-type acceleration mechanism.

The paper also clarifies what this mechanism is not. It is not a modular
transformation, and it is not the same mechanism as the Baker \(4{:}3\)
trisection cascade. The latter is a nonlinear residual-map iteration in the
signed angular error. Its local law is cubic in the current error, not a
fixed-resolution cancellation in \(N^{-2}\). The distinction is important:
scaling--cancellation increases the order of a fixed asymptotic expansion,
whereas the Baker cascade multiplies the local error order under iteration.

\section{Even-Power Asymptotic Structure}

Let \(Q\) be a geometric quantity associated with a smooth curve, polygonal
limit, or chord-based construction. Let \(Q_N\) be an approximation obtained
from \(N\) uniform samples, chords, or subdivisions.

The following assumption records the asymptotic structure used throughout
the paper.

\begin{assumption}[Even-power chord expansion]
The approximation \(Q_N\) admits an asymptotic expansion of the form
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
C_6N^{-6}
+
O(N^{-8}),
\]
where the constants \(C_{2j}\) depend on the underlying geometry but not on
\(N\).
\end{assumption}

This assumption is natural for symmetric chord-based approximations. For
example, if a smooth curve is sampled at uniform parameter spacing
\(h=1/N\), then Taylor expansion of the local chord contribution produces
powers of \(h\). Symmetry under reversal of the local interval cancels the
odd-order contributions, leaving even powers of \(h\), equivalently even
powers of \(N^{-1}\).

\begin{remark}
The paper does not require a universal proof that every chord-based
approximation has such an expansion. The scaling--cancellation theorem is
conditional on the displayed expansion. In applications, the expansion may
be proved by Taylor expansion, Euler--Maclaurin analysis, or standard
perimeter approximation estimates.
\end{remark}

\section{The Scaling Law}

The even-power expansion immediately gives a scaling law.

\begin{proposition}[Scaling of the leading errors]
Assume
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
C_6N^{-6}
+
O(N^{-8}).
\]
For a fixed integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
Q-Q_{kN}
=
\frac{C_2}{k^2}N^{-2}
+
\frac{C_4}{k^4}N^{-4}
+
\frac{C_6}{k^6}N^{-6}
+
O(N^{-8}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(kN\) for \(N\) in the asymptotic expansion:
\[
Q-Q_{kN}
=
C_2(kN)^{-2}
+
C_4(kN)^{-4}
+
C_6(kN)^{-6}
+
O((kN)^{-8}).
\]
Since \(k\) is fixed, this becomes
\[
Q-Q_{kN}
=
\frac{C_2}{k^2}N^{-2}
+
\frac{C_4}{k^4}N^{-4}
+
\frac{C_6}{k^6}N^{-6}
+
O(N^{-8}).
\]
\end{proof}

Thus the leading error coefficient is transformed by the predictable factor
\(k^{-2}\). This is the source of the cancellation formula.

\section{The Scaling--Cancellation Principle}

\begin{theorem}[Scaling--cancellation principle]
Suppose
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
For fixed \(k\geq2\), define
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
Q_{kN}
+
\frac{Q_{kN}-Q_N}{k^2-1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
\frac{k^2Q_{kN}-Q_N}{k^2-1}.
\]
Then
\[
Q-\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
O(N^{-4}).
\]
More precisely,
\[
Q-\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
-\frac{C_4}{k^2}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From the expansion,
\[
Q_N
=
Q
-
C_2N^{-2}
-
C_4N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}),
\]
and
\[
Q_{kN}
=
Q
-
\frac{C_2}{k^2}N^{-2}
-
\frac{C_4}{k^4}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
Using
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
\frac{k^2Q_{kN}-Q_N}{k^2-1},
\]
we compute
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}-Q
=
\frac{
k^2(Q_{kN}-Q)-(Q_N-Q)
}{
k^2-1
}.
\]
Now
\[
k^2(Q_{kN}-Q)
=
-C_2N^{-2}
-
\frac{C_4}{k^2}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}),
\]
while
\[
Q_N-Q
=
-C_2N^{-2}
-
C_4N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
Subtracting gives
\[
k^2(Q_{kN}-Q)-(Q_N-Q)
=
C_4\left(1-\frac{1}{k^2}\right)N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
Dividing by \(k^2-1\),
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}-Q
=
\frac{C_4}{k^2}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
Therefore
\[
Q-\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
-\frac{C_4}{k^2}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}),
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The combination
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
Q_{kN}
+
\frac{Q_{kN}-Q_N}{k^2-1}
\]
uses only the two approximations \(Q_N\) and \(Q_{kN}\). It does not require
knowledge of \(C_2\). The coefficient \(C_2\) is eliminated because its
scaling factor \(k^{-2}\) is known.
\end{remark}

\section{Higher-Order Fixed-Scale Cancellation}

The same idea extends to more than two scales. Suppose the full even-power
expansion has the form
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
C_6N^{-6}
+
\cdots .
\]

Choose distinct fixed scale factors
\[
k_0,k_1,\ldots,k_s,
\qquad
k_j>0,
\]
and form a linear combination
\[
\widehat Q_N^{(s)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_jQ_{k_jN}.
\]

To preserve the limiting value \(Q\), the weights must satisfy
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_j=1.
\]
To cancel the first \(s\) even-power error terms, require
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_jk_j^{-2\ell}=0,
\qquad
\ell=1,2,\ldots,s.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Higher-order fixed-scale cancellation]
Assume
\[
Q-Q_N
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{s+1}C_{2\ell}N^{-2\ell}
+
O(N^{-2s-4}).
\]
Let
\[
k_0=1,
\qquad
k_1,\ldots,k_s
\]
be distinct positive scale factors, and define
\[
\widehat Q_N^{(s)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_jQ_{k_jN}.
\]
Suppose the weights satisfy
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_j=1
\]
and
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_jk_j^{-2\ell}=0,
\qquad
\ell=1,2,\ldots,s.
\]
Then
\[
Q-\widehat Q_N^{(s)}
=
O(N^{-2s-2}).
\]

For distinct values
\[
k_0^{-2},k_1^{-2},\ldots,k_s^{-2},
\]
these conditions form a nonsingular Vandermonde system. Hence the weights
exist and are unique.
\end{theorem}

\section{Application to Polygonal Approximation of \texorpdfstring{\(\pi\)}{pi}}

For an inscribed regular \(N\)-gon in the unit circle, the perimeter is
\[
P_N
=
2N\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{N}\right).
\]
Using the sine expansion,
\[
\sin x
=
x-\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{x^5}{120}+O(x^7),
\]
we obtain
\[
P_N
=
2N
\left[
\frac{\pi}{N}
-
\frac{\pi^3}{6N^3}
+
\frac{\pi^5}{120N^5}
+
O(N^{-7})
\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
P_N
=
2\pi
-
\frac{\pi^3}{3}N^{-2}
+
\frac{\pi^5}{60}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]
Thus
\[
2\pi-P_N
=
\frac{\pi^3}{3}N^{-2}
-
\frac{\pi^5}{60}N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]

Applying the scaling--cancellation theorem gives
\[
\widehat P_{N,k}
=
P_{kN}
+
\frac{P_{kN}-P_N}{k^2-1},
\]
with
\[
2\pi-\widehat P_{N,k}
=
O(N^{-4}).
\]

For \(k=3\),
\[
\widehat P_{N,3}
=
P_{3N}
+
\frac{P_{3N}-P_N}{8}.
\]

\begin{remark}
This construction does not prove a new value of \(\pi\). It accelerates a
known convergent polygonal approximation by cancelling its leading
asymptotic error term.
\end{remark}

\section{Application to Smooth Convex-Curve Perimeters}

Let \(\Gamma\) be a smooth closed convex curve with perimeter \(P\), and let
\(P_N\) be a chordal perimeter approximation using \(N\) equal-parameter
samples. Under standard smoothness and symmetry assumptions, one obtains an
even-power expansion
\[
P-P_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}).
\]

The scaling--cancellation theorem gives
\[
\widehat P_{N,k}
=
P_{kN}
+
\frac{P_{kN}-P_N}{k^2-1},
\]
and therefore
\[
P-\widehat P_{N,k}
=
O(N^{-4}).
\]

For \(k=3\),
\[
\widehat P_{N,3}
=
P_{3N}
+
\frac{P_{3N}-P_N}{8}.
\]

This is the same structural mechanism used in chordal ellipse-perimeter
corrections. The method does not depend on the ellipse specifically; the
ellipse is a particularly important example because its exact perimeter is
non-elementary and classical approximations such as Ramanujan's formula act
on a different variable, namely eccentricity.

\section{Comparison with the Baker \texorpdfstring{\(4{:}3\)}{4:3} Trisection Cascade}

The preceding applications are fixed-resolution approximations. Their
active asymptotic variable is \(N^{-2}\), and the improvement is obtained
by combining approximants at \(N\), \(kN\), and possibly further fixed
scales.

The Baker \(4{:}3\) trisection cascade belongs to a different class. There
the active variable is not a discretization scale but the signed angular
error
\[
e_j
=
D_j-\frac{\theta}{3}.
\]
For the Baker \(4{:}3\) branch, the local residual map has the form
\[
e_{j+1}
=
-\frac{7}{384}e_j^3
+
O(e_j^5).
\]
After normalization, this becomes the degree-three normal form
\[
\eta_{j+1}
=
-\eta_j^3
+
O(\eta_j^5).
\]

Thus the trisection cascade is not a direct instance of the
scaling--cancellation theorem. It is a nonlinear residual-map iteration
whose local effect is to multiply the current error order by three:
\[
3
\longrightarrow
9
\longrightarrow
27
\longrightarrow
81
\longrightarrow
\cdots .
\]

The comparison is nevertheless useful. In both settings, geometry supplies
a distinguished coefficient, scaling, or correction that suppresses a
leading error contribution. But the mechanisms are logically distinct:

\begin{itemize}
\item scaling--cancellation is order-increasing cancellation in a
fixed-resolution parameter;
\item the Baker \(4{:}3\) cascade is order-multiplying nonlinear iteration in
the signed error variable.
\end{itemize}

This distinction prevents the fixed N-series mechanism from being mistaken
for a modular transformation or for the Baker cubic residual cascade.

\section{Discussion}

The scaling--cancellation mechanism has three ingredients:

\begin{itemize}
\item symmetry produces an even-power expansion;
\item scaling gives predictable transformations of the asymptotic terms;
\item linear combination cancels the leading error contribution.
\end{itemize}

This produces systematic order improvement without nonlinear iteration. In
the simplest case, a pair of approximants \(Q_N\) and \(Q_{kN}\) cancels the
leading \(N^{-2}\) term and produces fourth-order accuracy. With more fixed
scales, successive even-power terms may be cancelled.

The mechanism is therefore best described as a Richardson-type geometric
acceleration. It should be distinguished from super-exponential modular
iteration, such as the arithmetic--geometric mean, and from nonlinear
geometric residual cascades, such as the Baker \(4{:}3\) trisection
construction.

\section{Conclusion}

We have formulated a scaling--cancellation principle governing chord-based
geometric approximations with even-power asymptotic expansions. If
\[
Q-Q_N
=
C_2N^{-2}
+
C_4N^{-4}
+
O(N^{-6}),
\]
then
\[
\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
Q_{kN}
+
\frac{Q_{kN}-Q_N}{k^2-1}
\]
satisfies
\[
Q-\widehat Q_{N,k}
=
O(N^{-4}).
\]

The framework applies directly to polygonal approximations of \(\pi\),
chordal perimeter approximations for ellipses, and smooth convex-curve
perimeter approximations. It also clarifies the relationship between
geometric N-series acceleration and other refinement mechanisms.

The Baker \(4{:}3\) trisection cascade is not a scaling--cancellation
operator. It is a separate cubic residual-map cascade. Together, these
examples show two complementary forms of geometric error suppression:
order-increasing cancellation in a resolution variable and order-multiplying
iteration in an error variable.

Future work will extend the fixed-resolution scaling--cancellation framework
to higher-order combinations, nonuniform chord distributions, and broader
classes of smooth curves.
